Prettiest Species of Jellyfish

Lion's Mane

1st

The lion’s mane jelly is the largest sea jelly among the over 200 species that make up the class of the true jellies, Scyphozoa. The genus name, Cyanea, is derived from the Greek kyanos which means dark blue. The first species described in the genus was dark blue. The Latin capillus means hair and presumably describes this jelly’s many tentacles.

Black Sea Nettle

2nd

The black sea nettle is a large, dark-colored jellyfish from the Pacific Ocean, officially recognized only since 1997. It can grow to be over three feet wide with tentacles extending up to 25 feet. This species feeds on other jellyfish and zooplankton, and while its sting is painful, it is not considered dangerous to humans.

Crystal

3rd

Crystal jellyfish are transparent, bioluminescent invertebrates found in the Northern Pacific Ocean, with a bell up to 10 inches wide and delicate tentacles. They eat small crustaceans and other jellies, sometimes consuming prey larger than themselves by expanding their mouths.

Egg-yolk

4th

Egg-yolk jellyfish, also known as Phacellophora camtschatica, are large jellyfish with a bright yellow, central bell that resembles a fried egg and can grow up to two feet in diameter. These jellyfish are often found drifting in the open ocean and have long, sticky tentacles that can be up to 20 feet long, used to passively catch prey.

Firework

5th

The firework jellyfish is a deep-sea hydrozoan known for its stunning, starburst-like appearance, which is not bioluminescence but rather light reflecting off its body and tentacles. These jellyfish live in the mesopelagic and bathypelagic zones at depths of approximately 4,000 to 5,000 feet, near the Revillagigedo Archipelago off Mexico, and have been found in various oceans worldwide

Pink Meanie

6th

Drymonema larsoni, also known as the pink meanie, is a large jellyfish first identified in the early 2000s, with a bell up to 3 feet in diameter and tentacles that can reach 70 feet. This carnivorous species, found in the Gulf of Mexico, Mediterranean, and near South Africa, preys heavily on moon jellyfish, playing a key role in controlling their populations.
